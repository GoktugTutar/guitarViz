Guitar chord positions

A chord is a set of notes. A position is one way to place those notes under your fingers. Learn the difference, then find a shape you can play.

Try it on the fretboard →

Build a chord from its notes

A major chord uses degrees 1, 3 and 5 of its major scale. G major contains G, B and D. A minor chord lowers the third by a semitone: G minor contains G, B♭ and D.

Read a playable position

Choose Chord Finder, then select a root and chord type. Playable positions shows one fingering at a time. All note locations shows every matching note across the neck; those dots are choices, not a request to play them all together.

In the position table, strings run from 1 (high e) to 6 (low E). A 0 means play an open string and × means mute it. Finger numbers are 1 for index, 2 for middle, 3 for ring and 4 for pinky. A barre uses one finger across several strings.

Example: open C and an F barre chord

Open C major is ×–3–2–0–1–0 when written from low E to high e. Mute the lowest string, press fret 3 on A, fret 2 on D and fret 1 on B, and let G and high e ring open.

An E-shape F major chord is 1–3–3–2–1–1, also from low to high. The index finger forms a barre at fret 1. These shapes sound different because their notes sit in different registers, even when a note name is repeated.

Which shapes are included?

The current position library includes common open chords and movable E- and A-based shapes, with higher-octave repeats where they fit. It does not yet provide all five CAGED shape families for every chord. C, G and D appear in some open positions.

Standard tuning is E–A–D–G–B–e from low to high. Half-step-down positions compensate for the lower tuning. In Drop D, the Chord Finder mutes string 6 and uses verified positions on the upper strings.

Practice moving between two positions

  1. Choose a chord and play its first position slowly.
  2. Switch to Intervals to locate the root, third and fifth.
  3. Select another position and compare the order and register of the notes.
  4. Use Fingers for the fingering, then Play chord to hear it.
