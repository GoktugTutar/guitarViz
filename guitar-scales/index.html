Start with G major

The G major scale contains G, A, B, C, D, E and F♯. Its interval formula is 1–2–3–4–5–6–7. The first degree is the root: G. In the app, the root appears as R, so the display reads R–2–3–4–5–6–7.

In standard tuning, a low G is at fret 3 of string 6. Play G–A–B on that string at frets 3–5–7, then C–D–E on string 5 at frets 3–5–7, and F♯–G on string 4 at frets 4–5. This is one route through one octave; the same notes occur elsewhere on the neck.

Read a scale in TAB

The top line is the highest string. Each number tells you which fret to press. Read the notes from left to right. This G major example uses open strings and nearby frets:

Compare major, minor and pentatonic

Natural minor changes the third, sixth and seventh of the major formula: 1–2–♭3–4–5–♭6–♭7. For example, A natural minor is A–B–C–D–E–F–G. A minor pentatonic keeps five of those notes: A–C–D–E–G, with the formula 1–♭3–4–5–♭7.

Intervals describe a note relative to the selected root. The same C is the fourth degree in G major but the minor third in A minor. Changing the root changes that relationship.

Try it in the explorer

  1. Choose Scale, root G and Major.
  2. Switch between Notes, Intervals and TAB.
  3. Tap the yellow root notes. Then use Play scale to hear a one-octave sequence.
  4. Use Layer to compare the scale with a G major chord. G, B and D belong to both.

On a phone, swipe horizontally across the fretboard. The instrument stays in TAB orientation, with the high string at the top.
